**Auditor:** CitationGuard AI Research Agent  
**Artifacts Generated:** `dataset_statistics.json`, `dataset_audit_report.md`

## 1. Executive Summary

The objective of **CitationGuard AI** is to detect unsupported academic claims by evaluating natural language in-text citation contexts against the evidence presented in cited literature. To construct a scientifically defensible, evidence-aware citation verification pipeline, an audit of five candidate research datasets was conducted:

1. **SciClaim** (`SciClaim-Dataset-main`)
2. **SciFact** (`data/data`)
3. **MSVEC** (`msvec-main`)
4. **SciCite** (`scicite/scicite`)
5. **SCitance** (`scitance-main`)

### Key Findings at a Glance

* **Direct Citation Verification Core:** **SCitance** is the only dataset among the five that directly evaluates natural in-text citation sentences (*citances*) against cited scientific papers. However, SCitance has severe anomalies: **100% of its corpus documents (435/435)** have corrupted title metadata (the first sentence of the abstract was inserted into the `title` field), and **38.67% of its claims (251/649)** are synthetic negations generated by GPT-3.5.
* **Gold-Standard Claim Entailment Engine:** **SciFact** provides 1,409 atomic scientific claims and 5,183 peer-reviewed biomedical abstracts with sentence-level rationale annotations. While its claims are human-curated and decontextualized (not raw citation contexts), it serves as the premier source for fine-grained sentence-level evidence extraction and natural language inference (NLI).
* **Severe Cross-Dataset Leakage:** SCitance was derived directly from the citation sentences underlying SciFact. Consequently, **86.17% (81/94)** of the documents cited in the SCitance test set are present in the SciFact training set, and **25 claim pairs** share $\ge 70\%$ lexical Jaccard similarity. Merging SciFact train with SCitance test would cause massive data leakage.
* **Corpus Redundancy:** The MSVEC search corpus (5,244 papers) is **98.8% identical** to SciFact (5,183 shared documents). MSVEC contains only 56 novel fact-checked claims and functions exclusively as an out-of-domain evaluation benchmark.
* **Task Incompatibility (Do Not Naively Merge):** **SciCite** (11,020 core citations) annotates **citation intent** (`background`, `method`, `result`), not factual truthfulness. Mapping `method` or `background` to `unsupported` or `supported` is methodologically invalid. SciCite must remain strictly an auxiliary intent-filtering dataset.
* **Intra-Paper Claim Detection:** **SciClaim** (2,391 sentences from 15 bio-agriculture papers) annotates sentences as `Claim` (1), `Evidence` (2), or `None` (0). It lacks external citation links and cross-document verification, but provides supervised signals for in-text claim extraction. A data defect was uncovered: 22 sentences in its test set have conflicting labels between `SciClaim_sentences_test.tsv` and `SciClaim_sentences_test_withouT.tsv`.

## 3. Dataset-by-Dataset Analysis

### 3.1. SciClaim

```
Sample Record (train.tsv):
label: "0"
text: "(title:Exogenous fibrolytic enzymes to unlock nutrients: Histological investigation of its effects on fibre degradation in ruminants) There is a need for a better understanding of the mode-of-action of exogenous fibrolytic enzymes (EFE) used as additives in ruminant feeds."
```

1. **Original Task:** Fine-grained recognition of scientific claims and evidence sentences across all full-text paper sections (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion) in the bio-agriculture domain.
2. **Unit of Record:** A single sentence from a scientific paper. In train, val, and test, each sentence is prefixed by the paper's title: `(title:<Title>) <Sentence>`.
3. **Labels:** Integer strings `"0"`, `"1"`, `"2"`.
4. **Label Meanings:**
   * `"1"` (**Claim**): Propositions expressing core findings, relationships, or comparisons (e.g., *(A, B, relationship)*).
   * `"2"` (**Evidence**): Concrete experimental quantitative measurements or citations of theoretical models supporting a claim.
   * `"0"` (**None-type**): Background context, objectives, methodological setup, or future work suggestions.
5. **Direct Relation to Citation Verification:** **No.** It does not contain in-text citations, citation markers, cited document links, or external evidence retrieval.
6. **Relation to Scientific Claim Verification:** **Yes.** Directly models the upstream stage of *claim identification* (distinguishing whether a sentence constitutes an assertion that requires proof).
7. **Relation to Evidence Retrieval:** **Partial/Intra-paper.** Identifies evidence sentences within the same paper, but does not retrieve external evidence for a citation.
8. **Relation to Citation Intent:** **No.**
9. **Utility for CitationGuard AI:** Highly useful for training the **Claim Extractor / Filter**, ensuring the pipeline only verifies sentences that assert factual claims rather than background context or methods.
10. **Limitations:**
    * Limited domain coverage: only 15 papers in bio-agriculture.
    * Small scale: 2,391 sentences total.
    * Text formatting artifact: prefix `(title:...)` must be cleaned.
    * Critical label discrepancy: 22 sentences in `test.tsv` have different labels compared to `test_withouT.tsv`.

### 3.2. SciFact

```
Sample Record (claims_train.jsonl):
{
  "id": 2,
  "claim": "1 in 5 million in UK have abnormal PrP positivity.",
  "evidence": {
    "13734012": [{"sentences": [4], "label": "CONTRADICT"}]
  },
  "cited_doc_ids": [13734012]
}
```

1. **Original Task:** Scientific claim verification against biomedical research abstracts, comprising document retrieval, sentence rationale selection, and stance classification.
2. **Unit of Record:** An atomic, human-written claim proposition mapped to cited candidate documents, rationale sentence indices within candidate abstracts, and stance labels.
3. **Labels:** Stance labels at the document/rationale level: `"SUPPORT"`, `"CONTRADICT"`, and `"NO_EVIDENCE"` (implicit when `evidence` dictionary is empty).
4. **Label Meanings:**
   * `"SUPPORT"`: The cited paper abstract contains sentences that explicitly entail and confirm the claim.
   * `"CONTRADICT"`: The cited paper abstract contains sentences that explicitly refute the claim.
   * `"NO_EVIDENCE"`: The candidate abstract does not contain sufficient information to support or refute the claim.
5. **Direct Relation to Citation Verification:** **Moderate/Indirect.** The claims were derived from citation contexts in S2ORC, but were transformed by expert annotators into standalone, decontextualized factual statements.
6. **Relation to Scientific Claim Verification:** **Yes.** It is the primary benchmark for scientific claim verification.
7. **Relation to Evidence Retrieval:** **Yes, gold-standard.** Provides exact sentence indices within abstracts (`"sentences": [4]`) as ground-truth rationales.
8. **Relation to Citation Intent:** **No.**
9. **Utility for CitationGuard AI:** Fundamental for training the **Evidence-Aware Rationale Extractor** and the **Claim-Evidence Entailment Engine**.
10. **Limitations:**
    * Claims are idealized atomic sentences, lacking the discourse complexity, hedges, and citations of real academic writing.
    * `claims_test.jsonl` is unlabeled (test labels withheld for benchmark evaluation).
    * Minor internal duplicates and cross-split claim leakage (e.g., ID 871 in train and ID 870 in dev).

### 3.3. MSVEC

```
Sample Record (msvec.json):
{
  "id": 2,
  "claim": "Populations of wild deer across North America have tested positive for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies.",
  "doc_ids": [2],
  "evidence": {
    "2": [
      {"sentences": [4], "label": "SUPPORT"},
      {"sentences": [5], "label": "SUPPORT"}
    ]
  }
}
```

1. **Original Task:** Robustness evaluation of scientific claim verification systems (MultiVerS, GPT-3.5) against real-world, out-of-domain claims from fact-checking websites.
2. **Unit of Record:** A public fact-checked claim from Snopes or PolitiFact, linked to an academic research paper DOI and sentence-level evidence rationales.
3. **Labels:** SCV labels: `"SUPPORT"`, `"CONTRADICT"`, `"NO_EVIDENCE"`. Also provides raw fact-checker ratings (`"True"`, `"False"`, `"Mixture"`, `"Mostly False"`) in `dataset.txt`.
4. **Label Meanings:** Identical to SciFact for SCV labels.
5. **Direct Relation to Citation Verification:** **Low/Analagous.** Evaluates public scientific claims against referenced scientific literature, but does not model academic peer-to-peer citations.
6. **Relation to Scientific Claim Verification:** **Yes.** Serves as a zero-shot, out-of-domain evaluation set.
7. **Relation to Evidence Retrieval:** **Yes.** Claims must be verified against a 5,244-paper candidate corpus.
8. **Relation to Citation Intent:** **No.**
9. **Utility for CitationGuard AI:** Serves as an independent **Robustness / Stress-Test Evaluation Suite**.
10. **Limitations:**
    * Extremely small size: only 56 claims.
    * 98.8% of the search corpus is an exact copy of the SciFact corpus.
    * Journalistic claims differ stylistically from scholarly citations.

### 3.4. SciCite

```
Sample Record (train.jsonl):
{
  "source": "explicit",
  "citingPaperId": "1872080baa7d30ec8fb87be9a65358cd3a7fb649",
  "citedPaperId": "894be9b4ea46a5c422e81ef3c241072d4c73fdc0",
  "string": "However, how frataxin interacts with the Fe-S cluster biosynthesis components remains unclear as direct one-to-one interactions with each component were reported (IscS [12,22], IscU/Isu1 [6,11,16] or ISD11/Isd11 [14,15]).",
  "label": "background",
  "sectionName": "Introduction",
  "citeStart": 168,
  "citeEnd": 175,
  "isKeyCitation": true,
  "id": "1872080baa7d30ec8fb87be9a65358cd3a7fb649>894be9b4ea46a5c422e81ef3c241072d4c73fdc0",
  "unique_id": "1872080baa7d30ec8fb87be9a65358cd3a7fb649>894be9b4ea46a5c422e81ef3c241072d4c73fdc0_11"
}
```

1. **Original Task:** Citation intent recognition in academic texts, classifying why a paper was cited using structural scaffolds (section titles and cite-worthiness).
2. **Unit of Record:** An authentic in-text citation sentence (`string`) with character offsets to the citation marker (`citeStart`, `citeEnd`), citing paper ID, cited paper ID, and section name.
3. **Labels:** Primary intent: `"background"`, `"method"`, `"result"`. Secondary fine-grained stance: `"label2"` (e.g., `"supportive"`, highly sparse).
4. **Label Meanings:**
   * `"background"`: Cites work for domain overview, motivation, or prior context.
   * `"method"`: Cites work to adopt or modify a tool, library, dataset, or protocol.
   * `"result"`: Cites work to compare, validate, or contrast experimental findings.
5. **Direct Relation to Citation Verification:** **Orthogonal / Auxiliary.** Identifies the *rhetorical purpose* of a citation, not its factual veracity.
6. **Relation to Scientific Claim Verification:** **No.** It does not verify whether the cited paper supports what the author claimed.
7. **Relation to Evidence Retrieval:** **No.** Does not annotate evidence passages or abstracts.
8. **Relation to Citation Intent:** **Yes, authoritative.** The standard benchmark for citation intent classification.
9. **Utility for CitationGuard AI:** Crucial for **Intent Filtering / Routing**. Citations classified as `method` or purely general `background` rarely make verifiable factual claims and should be routed away from the entailment verifier to prevent false positive flags.
10. **Limitations:**
    * Cannot be merged into verification labels. Mapping `method` or `background` to `unsupported` or `supported` is invalid.
    * Identifiers are 40-character Semantic Scholar SHA hashes, incompatible with SciFact/SCitance integer IDs.
    * Missing `sectionName` in 724 instances.

### 3.5. SCitance

```
Sample Record (train.jsonl):
{
  "id": 1057,
  "claim": "Using manuipulations that enhance cSMAC formation, it was shown that cSMAC formation could enhance signaling by weak ligands (Cemerski et al. 2007) .",
  "evidence": {
    "306006": [{"label": "SUPPORT"}]
  },
  "citance_id": 8316253,
  "doc_ids": [306006]
}
```

1. **Original Task:** Zero-shot scientific claim verification using LLMs on raw, in-text citation sentences (*citances*) extracted from academic papers.
2. **Unit of Record:** A natural in-text citation sentence (or its synthetically generated negation) paired with cited document IDs and a document-level stance label (`SUPPORT` or `CONTRADICT`).
3. **Labels:** `"SUPPORT"`, `"CONTRADICT"`, and `"NO_EVIDENCE"` (when `evidence` is empty).
4. **Label Meanings:**
   * `"SUPPORT"`: The cited paper's abstract supports the claim made in the in-text citation sentence.
   * `"CONTRADICT"`: The cited paper refutes the claim (almost entirely synthetic negations).
   * `"NO_EVIDENCE"`: The cited paper does not provide sufficient evidence.
5. **Direct Relation to Citation Verification:** **YES, Direct Match.** Evaluates genuine in-text citation sentences citing target documents.
6. **Relation to Scientific Claim Verification:** **Yes.** Formulates citation verification as scholarly NLI.
7. **Relation to Evidence Retrieval:** **Yes, document-level.** Identifies relevant document IDs, but lacks sentence-level rationale annotations.
8. **Relation to Citation Intent:** **Implicit.** All records are citances, but explicit intent labels are not provided.
9. **Utility for CitationGuard AI:** The primary **Core Citation Verification Benchmark** reflecting realistic academic writing.
10. **Limitations:**
    * **Severe Metadata Corruption:** In `corpus.jsonl`, all 435 document records have the first sentence of their abstract placed into the `title` field.
    * **Synthetic Negation Bias:** 38.67% of claims (251/649) are synthetic LLM-generated negations rather than natural academic contradictions.
    * **Heavy Document Leakage with SciFact:** 86.17% of cited documents in SCitance test are present in SciFact train.
    * No sentence-level rationale annotations (only document-level labels).

## 5. Label Comparison

A critical requirement of this audit is to prevent forced or methodologically invalid label mappings.

```
                    ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │            LABEL SEMANTIC ONTOLOGY            │
                    └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐                       ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│     EPISTEMIC VERIFICATION      │                       │        RHETORICAL INTENT        │
│   (SciFact, SCitance, MSVEC)    │                       │       (SciCite, SciClaim)       │
└─────────────────────────────────┘                       └─────────────────────────────────┘
  ├── SUPPORT (Entailed by text)                            ├── SciCite: background, method,
  ├── CONTRADICT (Refuted by text)                          │            result
  └── NO_EVIDENCE (Insufficient info)                       └── SciClaim: Claim (1),
                                                                          Evidence (2),
                                                                          None-type (0)
```

### Label Mapping Feasibility Matrix

| Source Dataset | Source Label | Proposed Target Category | Semantic Validity | Methodological Justification & Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SciFact** | `SUPPORT` | `VERIFIED_SUPPORT` | **High** | Ground-truth paper explicitly entails claim with sentence rationales. |
| **SciFact** | `CONTRADICT` | `VERIFIED_REFUTE` | **High** | Ground-truth paper explicitly refutes claim with sentence rationales. |
| **SciFact** | `NO_EVIDENCE` | `UNSUPPORTED_NEI` | **High** | Standard neutral / not enough info class. |
| **SCitance** | `SUPPORT` | `VERIFIED_SUPPORT` | **High** | Cited paper supports the in-text citation claim. |
| **SCitance** | `CONTRADICT` | `VERIFIED_REFUTE` | **Medium** | Valid refutation label, but 100% of these in SCitance are synthetic LLM negations. |
| **SCitance** | `NO_EVIDENCE` | `UNSUPPORTED_NEI` | **High** | Cited paper lacks evidence. |
| **MSVEC** | `SUPPORT`/`CONTRADICT`| Evaluation Stance | **High** | Valid, but keep separated as out-of-domain benchmark. |
| **SciCite** | `background` | *Do not map to stance* | **INVALID** | A background citation is often fully supported by the cited paper. Mapping to "unsupported" creates false positives. |
| **SciCite** | `method` | *Do not map to stance* | **INVALID** | Citing a tool or protocol has no epistemic truth value regarding claim verification. |
| **SciCite** | `result` | *Do not map to stance* | **INVALID** | Indicates an empirical comparison, but does not specify whether the comparison is supported or unsupported. |
| **SciClaim** | `1` (Claim) | `ROLE_CLAIM` | **High** | Indicates sentence asserts a finding; suitable for claim extraction, not verification stance. |
| **SciClaim** | `2` (Evidence) | `ROLE_EVIDENCE` | **High** | Indicates sentence presents experimental evidence; suitable for rationale identification. |
| **SciClaim** | `0` (None) | `ROLE_NEUTRAL` | **High** | Contextual text. |

## 6. Data Quality Analysis

### 6.1. SciClaim Quality Audit
* **Total Standard Records:** 2,391 sentences across 15 full papers.
* **Missing Values:** 0 missing labels, 0 missing texts.
* **Label Distribution:**
  * Train: `0`: 800 (48.28%), `1`: 482 (29.09%), `2`: 375 (22.63%)
  * Val: `0`: 167 (40.73%), `1`: 132 (32.20%), `2`: 111 (27.07%)
  * Test: `0`: 162 (50.00%), `2`: 85 (26.23%), `1`: 77 (23.77%)
* **Identified Defects & Anomalies:**
  1. **Test Split Discrepancy:** Comparing `SciClaim_sentences_test.tsv` against `SciClaim_sentences_test_withouT.tsv` reveals **22 conflicting labels** on identical sentences (e.g., Row 1 is labeled `1` in `test.tsv` and `2` in `test_withouT.tsv`; Row 89 is `1` vs `0`; Row 91 is `2` vs `1`). The official `README.md` statistics match `test.tsv`, meaning `test_withouT.tsv` is corrupted.
  2. **Validation Duplicate Sentences:** 20 duplicate sentences exist in `val.tsv` due to repeated figure captions and headers across sections.
  3. **Prefix Pollution:** Every sentence in train, val, and test contains `(title:<Paper Title>) ` prepended to the string.

### 6.2. SciFact Quality Audit
* **Total Claims:** 1,409 (Train: 809, Dev: 300, Test: 300).
* **Missing Values:** 0 missing fields in train and dev. (Test split has missing `evidence` and `cited_doc_ids` by benchmark design).
* **Label Distribution (Train Evidence Instances):**
  * `SUPPORT`: 616 rationales
  * `CONTRADICT`: 341 rationales
  * `NO_EVIDENCE`: 304 claims
* **Identified Defects & Anomalies:**
  1. **Internal Duplicate Claims:**
     * Train: `"Adult tissue-resident macrophages are seeded before birth."` and `"Adult tissue-resident macrophages stem from the embryonal yolk sac and fetal liver."` appear twice with different IDs.
     * Test: `"Hematopoietic progenitor cells are never susceptible to HIV-1 infection ex vivo."`, `"Obesity decreases life expectancy."`, and `"Obesity prolongs life expectancy."` appear twice.
  2. **Cross-Split Claim Leakage:**
     * Train & Dev overlap: `"Obesity decreases life quality."` (ID 871 in train, ID 870 in dev) and `"There is no association between HNF4A mutations and diabetes risks."` (ID 1291 in train, ID 1292 in dev).
     * Train & Test overlap: `"Adult tissue-resident macrophages possess a self-renewing capacity."` (ID 88 in train, ID 87 in test).

### 6.3. MSVEC Quality Audit
* **Total Claims:** 56 claims.
* **Corpus Size:** 5,244 papers.
* **Label Distribution:** 53 `SUPPORT` rationale instances, 42 `CONTRADICT` instances, 20 `NO_EVIDENCE` claims.
* **Identified Defects & Anomalies:**
  1. **Redundant Corpus:** 5,183 out of 5,244 documents (98.8%) are byte-for-byte identical duplicates of SciFact's corpus.
  2. **Sample Size:** At $N=56$, the dataset cannot support model fine-tuning; it is purely an evaluation probe.

### 6.4. SciCite Quality Audit
* **Total Core Records:** 11,020 (Train: 8,243, Dev: 916, Test: 1,861).
* **Missing Values:**
  * `sectionName`: Missing in **724 records** (Train: 587, Dev: 71, Test: 66).
  * `label2`: Missing in **9,849 records** (present in only 1,171 records across all splits).
  * `label_confidence`: Missing in **4,099 records**.
* **Label Distribution:**
  * Train: `background`: 4,840 (58.72%), `method`: 2,294 (27.83%), `result`: 1,109 (13.45%)
  * Dev: `background`: 538 (58.73%), `method`: 255 (27.84%), `result`: 123 (13.43%)
  * Test: `background`: 997 (53.57%), `method`: 605 (32.51%), `result`: 259 (13.92%)
* **Identified Defects & Anomalies:**
  1. **Class Imbalance:** Strong imbalance favoring `background` (~58%) over `result` (~13.5%).
  2. **Cross-Split Duplicate Strings:** 2 citation strings appear in both `train.jsonl` and `dev.jsonl`.

### 1. The SciFact–SCitance Provenance Leakage (High Severity)
Inspection of `code/dataset_generation.ipynb` in SCitance proved that SCitance was constructed by taking the citation sentences (*citances*) in S2ORC that originally gave rise to SciFact claims. 
* **Lexical Near-Duplicates:** 25 claim pairs between SciFact and SCitance share $\ge 70\%$ token Jaccard similarity:
  * *SCitance (ID 31):* `"Recently a strong bias in the phage genome locations where the spacers were derived has been observed in many CRISPR subtypes that confer the immunity to phage ."`
  * *SciFact (ID 47):* `"A strong bias in the phage genome locations where the spacers were derived has been observed in many CRISPR subtypes that confer the immunity to phage."*
  * *SCitance (ID 434):* `"However, HSCT-T dosage may not be diagnostic if the onset of symptoms occurs less than 3 hours before AMI (42) ."`
  * *SciFact (ID 494):* `"HSCT-T dosage may not be diagnostic if the onset of symptoms occurs less than 3 hours before AMI."*
* **Split Contamination:** If SciFact `train` is used to train an entailment model and SCitance `test` is used to evaluate citation verification, **81 out of 94 cited documents (86.17%)** in the test set will have been seen during training. This inflates evaluation scores and obscures out-of-distribution failure modes.

### 2. MSVEC–SciFact Corpus Identity (Medium Severity)
MSVEC's corpus contains 5,244 papers, of which 5,183 (98.8%) are exact duplicates of SciFact's corpus. When evaluating MSVEC, models trained on SciFact are operating on in-corpus retrieval.

## 11. Problems, Anomalies, and Critical Warnings

### ⚠️ Warning 1: SCitance Corpus Metadata Corruption
In `scitance-main/data/scitance/corpus.jsonl`, all 435 documents have their `title` field set to the first sentence of the `abstract`.  
* **Remedy:** Do not use `corpus.jsonl` from SCitance. Because all 435 document IDs exist in SciFact's `corpus.jsonl`, join SCitance against SciFact's `corpus.jsonl` using `doc_id` to restore true paper titles.

### ⚠️ Warning 2: SciClaim Test Label Discrepancy
`SciClaim_sentences_test.tsv` and `SciClaim_sentences_test_withouT.tsv` have conflicting labels on 22 rows.  
* **Remedy:** Discard `SciClaim_sentences_test_withouT.tsv`. Use `SciClaim_sentences_test.tsv` (which matches the author's published paper and README statistics), and programmatically strip the `(title:...) ` prefix using a regex parser.

### ⚠️ Warning 3: Artificial Negation Bias in SCitance
38.67% of SCitance claims are synthetic LLM negations (e.g., insertion of "does not", "fails to").  
* **Remedy:** In CitationGuard evaluation, report performance disaggregated by **Natural Refutations** vs **Synthetic Negations** to avoid misleading claim verification accuracy.

### ⚠️ Warning 4: The Fallacy of Mapping Citation Intent to Verification Stance
Mapping SciCite labels (`background`, `method`, `result`) into `SUPPORT`/`CONTRADICT` is scientifically invalid. Citations labeled `background` or `method` are frequently legitimate, supported citations. They simply lack verifiable empirical claims.  
* **Remedy:** Use SciCite only to train an Intent Classifier that discards or flags citations that do not warrant evidence retrieval.

## 12. Recommendation for the Next Phase

### A. Dataset Suitable for Core Citation Verification
* **SCitance** is the primary dataset representing genuine citation verification, as its inputs are in-text citation sentences citing target literature.

### B. Dataset Suitable for Claim Verification
* **SciFact** is the premier dataset for scientific claim verification, offering rigorous, human-curated claims and sentence rationales.

### C. Dataset Suitable for Evidence / Entailment
* **SciFact** provides the required sentence-level rationale annotations (`"sentences": [4]`) necessary to train an evidence-aware rationale extraction and entailment module. (SCitance only provides document-level labels).

### D. Datasets Suitable Only as Auxiliary Data
* **SciCite:** Auxiliary dataset for training a Citation Intent Classifier to filter out non-claim citations (`method`, `background`).
* **SciClaim:** Auxiliary dataset for training a Claim Detector to locate verifiable propositions within cited paragraphs.
* **MSVEC:** Auxiliary out-of-domain benchmark to evaluate model robustness on real-world scientific misinformation.

### E. Datasets That Should NOT Be Merged
* **SciCite must NOT be merged with SciFact or SCitance.** Their label sets are semantically disjoint.
* **SciClaim must NOT be merged with SciFact or SCitance.** Its task is sentence classification within a single paper, not cross-paper citation verification.
* **MSVEC must NOT be merged into training splits.** It consists of only 56 claims and should remain a hold-out test set.
